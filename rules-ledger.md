# Rules Ledger — How This Blog Is Written

_Status: `LIVING`. Created 2026-10-02. Last updated: 2026-10-02._

This is the standing rulebook for everything published on this blog and
everything drafted for it. It adapts rules from the micro-SaaS planning
workspace (`../microsaas/docs/rules-ledger.md` and the voice docs it
cites). Those rules were written for a product and its marketing. The
versions here are rewritten for **one parent writing in public about his own
family**. Each rule names its source so the full reasoning can be found.

This ledger governs this blog. If a rule here and the microsaas source
disagree, this file decides what happens on the blog. Fix the wording here
deliberately and date the change. Don't quietly copy the source over it.

**Maintenance rule:** any commit that adds, changes, or retires a rule
updates this file in the same commit, dated `YYYY-MM-DD`.

## 1. Her — the person who can't consent

She is the subject of much of this blog, and she can't agree to any of it.
That raises the bar on every rule below.

- **Public is forever, so decide before writing, not after.** Before the
  first post that tells her story, three decisions get made and dated in
  this file's §7. (a) **Her name in public**: her name, an initial, *our
  daughter*, or a chosen name. (b) **A written withdrawal promise**: what
  happens if she one day wants a post taken down, including the honest
  limits (search caches, archives, other people's copies). (c) **Her
  mother's own yes**, because *we* makes her a subject too. Until (a) is
  decided, every draft uses `[daughter]`. Her real name never appears in
  this repository, drafts and ideas included.
  ← `../microsaas/docs/vision/voice/who-is-behind-it.md` §5;
  microsaas ledger §5 *public is forever* `[her][people][process]`
- **Her worth carries the post, never her deficits.** I don't trade on
  what she can't do for effect. A hard truth can be told when it serves
  her being known. It is never told as a hook.
  ← microsaas ledger §5 *the child's worth, never her deficits* `[her][register]`
- **She is not a mascot, and not a lesson.** She isn't the reason for
  anything else I'm doing, and she isn't here to teach readers something.
  ← `who-is-behind-it.md` §3.2; microsaas ledger §8 *the guest test* `[her][register]`
- **Write it so she could read it at twenty-five.** If a sentence would
  embarrass her as an adult, or describe her body, behavior, or medical
  detail she would reasonably want private, cut it or make it general.
  The general claim usually lands without the private detail.
  ← `../microsaas/docs/outreach/voice-and-messaging.md` §the flag, guard 3
  `[her]`
- **What she expressed is hers, and it's recognized, not decoded.** When I
  write about something she told us (by AAC, gesture, choosing between two
  things, a look held), I write it as her act. I don't claim we "gave her a
  voice"; she has one. The payoff stays on her (*"her first words,
  kept"*). It does not land on the audience (*"you'll be amazed what she
  was saying"*). ← `voice-and-messaging.md` §the flag, guards 2 and 4
  `[her][register]`
- **Date everything.** What's true at six is wrong at fourteen. Every post
  carries its date, and a post that has become wrong gets a dated note,
  not a silent edit. ← `../microsaas/docs/outreach/day-one-articles.md` §6
  `[her][claims]`

## 2. The register — honor, never pity

*Register* means the tone and posture of the writing: whom a sentence
looks at, and what it assumes about them. It is the most important
section of this ledger.

### 2a. The core rule

- **Honor, never pity.** Every sentence speaks in the register of honor:
  worth that is given, not earned. Pity accepts the verdict of "a lesser
  life" and adds sympathy, and pity-shaped writing never ships. The test
  for any sentence is **which direction does it look?** Up at her, or down
  at her? ← microsaas ledger §5 *honor, never pity*; `../microsaas/docs/vision/voice/pity.md`
  `[register]`
- **She is a person with a Tuesday, not a condition with a prognosis.**
  Write about the present, about specific things, about what she did.
  *"She got the zipper"* can only be written by someone who was there, and
  that specificity is the register. ← `pity.md` §what makes the right column
  work, tests 1–3 `[register]`
- **Ask for the ordinary, never the positive.** I don't write as if every
  post owes the reader a good ending or a bright side. A flat, tired, or
  hard day is a true day and can be written as one. Forced positivity is
  pity's twin. Pity calls the life a tragedy. Forced positivity calls the
  tragedy unwelcome. ← microsaas ledger §5 *ask for the ordinary* `[register]`

### 2b. About myself and our family

- **Never the saint script pointed at us.** No *brave*, no *despite*, no
  *I don't know how we do it*, no hero arc. If I write about myself in a
  register I'd object to about another family, every reader learns to run
  it on families like ours. The test: am I a person with a Tuesday, or a
  lesson? ← microsaas ledger §5 *a person is behind this*; `who-is-behind-it.md` §3.1
  `[register]`
- **"Journey" is allowed as a plain word, never as an arc.** The blog
  follows our life over time, and that's fine. What's ruled out is the
  *founder-story* or *inspiration* shape: the struggle, the turn, the
  triumph. ← `day-one-articles.md` §5 *founder origin-story arc*
  `[register]`
- **No pain as proof.** The hardest parts of our life aren't credentials.
  They're told only when telling them serves her or serves a reader who
  needs to hear it. ← `who-is-behind-it.md` §6 `[register][her]`
- **I know our Tuesday, not yours.** I never write *"we understand what
  you're going through"* to other families. I write what happened to us,
  in the first person. ← `who-is-behind-it.md` §6 `[register]`

### 2c. Addressed to other parents

- **I never preach to a parent about their own life.** Posts that may be
  read by another parent never tell them their life is the real one, that
  their child is anyone's teacher, or that their abnormal is the new
  normal. Read in an acute season, that sentence tells someone their
  crisis is fine. Arguments about inclusion, worth and the abled are
  aimed at **the people outside the door** (friends, church, strangers),
  never at the family inside it. ← microsaas ledger §5 *we never preach to you about
  your own life*; `../microsaas/docs/vision/voice/inclusion-inversion.md` §7
  `[register]`
- **Never tell a tired parent how to manage their friends.** The fix for
  isolation isn't more work for the most tired person in it. If I write
  about something I changed in myself (e.g. the *"I have to"* habit), I
  write it as mine, in the indicative, not as homework.
  ← `day-one-articles.md` §5 `[register]`
- **Indicative, not imperative.** Say what something turned out to be for
  us, not what it requires of the reader. ← `day-one-articles.md` §1
  `[register]`

### 2d. Addressed to friends, family and onlookers

- **The Tuesday test.** For any piece about *how to be around a family like
  ours*, does it make a nervous friend **more** likely to say something on
  Tuesday, or less? A piece written a half-turn too far becomes a list of
  ways to fail and freezes the reader it was written for. So:
  - it is written **to the reader about themselves**, never as a list they
    could hand to someone else;
  - nothing in it can be used as *"you shouldn't have said that"*;
  - it leaves **more sentences to say than to avoid**;
  - it **ends on permission**. A version that ends on the list of
    wrong phrases has done net harm.
  ← microsaas ledger §8 *the Tuesday test*; `../microsaas/docs/outreach/post-what-to-say.md`
  §editorial rules `[register][people]`
- **The guest test.** Does this make her a **host** or a **lesson**? A host
  has a house, tastes, a door, and the right not to be in the mood. A lesson
  is a device for the visitor's improvement. The tell is the payoff
  clause. If the benefit lands on the visitor's growth, rewrite it.
  *"Come and be with her, bring nothing"* passes. *"Spend time with her and
  you'll learn what really matters"* fails. A reader may say she changed
  them. I never say she is **for** changing them.
  ← microsaas ledger §8 *the guest test* `[register][her]`
- **Never correct anyone's words in public.** Almost every wrong phrase is
  said by someone who means well. Register is passed on by example, never
  enforced. No post quotes a real friend's or relative's misstep to make
  a point. ← microsaas ledger §5 *never correct a member's words*; `pity.md`
  §the two registers, preamble `[register][people]`
- **Specific offers, not open doors.** When I write about help, I describe
  the kind that names a day and a task (*"I'm free Saturday morning, can I
  take her to the park?"*). *"Let me know if you need anything"* puts the
  work on the person with the least capacity. ← `pity.md` §offers that
  aren't offers `[register][people]`

### 2e. Words

- **Our words for her are our choice, and other families' are theirs.**
  Identity-first (*disabled*), person-first (*with a disability*), or just
  her name. I pick for her, say why if it matters, and never correct
  another family's choice. ← microsaas ledger §3 *the family's words, not ours*
  `[register]`
- **The category's nouns stay out of my prose** unless I'm quoting a form:

  | Avoid | Use |
  |---|---|
  | "care recipient", "the client" | her name or `[daughter]` |
  | "caregiver burden" | what it took |
  | "support network" | the people around her, by name or role |
  | "compliance", "progress toward goals" | what she did; what she's working on |
  | "respite" (in my own voice) | someone else had her for a few hours |
  | "rare disease" as an introduction | the condition's name, after she's been introduced as a person |

  ← `pity.md` §our own copy — the category's nouns; `voice-and-messaging.md`
  §"rare journey" `[register]`
- **The ability pun never lands on her.** The blog's name plays on
  *inevitability*: the one ability every parent of a dependent child has to
  build is preparing for their own absence. The pun is safe only while the
  ability belongs to the reader or me. No *the only disability is…*,
  no *diffability*, no *superpower*. ← `voice-and-messaging.md`
  §"Inevitability" and misterinevitable.com `[register]`
- **Mind the Thanos line.** *"I am inevitable"* will come to some readers'
  minds. Self-aware humor about it is fine on this blog. It never goes
  near a post about grief or death. ← same section, *ways not to use it*
  item 3 `[register]`

### 2f. Fear, death and the faith register

- **Point at the preventable loss, never the unpreventable one.** A
  parent's death can't be prevented. What can be prevented is everything
  known about her dying with us. Posts about the years after us point at
  the second loss. ← microsaas ledger §5 *fear appeals only where purchase prevents
  the loss*; `voice-and-messaging.md` §misterinevitable, item 6 `[register]`
- **No statistics on life expectancy or caregiver turnover** as rhetoric.
  A number resizes something whose real size is the point.
  ← `day-one-articles.md` §5 `[register][claims]`
- **Faith pieces are their own pieces.** Our faith is real and it shapes
  how I see her worth (made in God's image, with no ability clause). It
  gets its own posts, written for readers who come to them. It doesn't
  get sprinkled into posts meant for a mixed audience. In those pieces, the
  weak party in the verse is **me, the reader**, never her (*my grace is
  sufficient* is not about her deficits). ← `voice-and-messaging.md`
  §register caution; `day-one-articles.md` §5 the faith-register row;
  `pity.md` §the faith register `[register]`

## 3. Other people

- **Nobody else is named without their yes.** Friends, relatives,
  therapists, teachers, church members: "a friend" by default.
  ← microsaas ledger §5 *named-helper consent* `[people]`
- **My wife gets her own yes,** for any post about her or written as
  *we*. *I* and *we* are different posts. ← `who-is-behind-it.md` §5c
  `[people]`
- **Siblings are people with their own privacy.** No sibling story goes
  up without thinking about what they'd want at twenty-five. Never cast a
  sibling as a helper or a lesson. ← `../microsaas/docs/vision/voice/siblings.md`
  `[people][her]`
- **Nothing about anyone else's child, ever,** including other PMS
  families we know. ← `who-is-behind-it.md` §6 `[people]`

## 4. Claims, advice and links

- **Peer experience, never advice.** I write what we did, what happened,
  and when. I don't write what another family should do. Benefits, legal
  documents, guardianship and trusts get *"this is what we did. Check
  your situation with someone qualified."* ← microsaas ledger §5 *peer experience,
  never advice* `[claims]`
- **No treatments, diets, protocols, or clinics** are recommended or
  linked, whatever the disclaimer. A link is an endorsement.
  ← `post-what-to-say.md` §link policy `[claims]`
- **No causal overclaims.** "We changed X and she slept better that
  month" is fine. "X fixes sleep in PMS" is not. ← `voice-and-messaging.md`
  §the flag, guard 2 `[claims]`
- **Verify every link at publication, and prefer the durable.** Link
  disabled adults writing about themselves, not only parents writing
  about children. Never link anything that raises money with pity
  imagery. ← `post-what-to-say.md` §link policy `[claims][register]`

## 5. Separation from the business

This is a personal blog. The micro-SaaS (`../microsaas`) may later host it
or link to it, but they are different things with different rules.

- **Nothing here offers the product.** No signup link, no waitlist, no
  pitch. A post that offers the product turns the blog into a business
  page. ← `voice-and-messaging.md` §misterinevitable, item 5;
  `../microsaas/docs/rules-ledger.md` §6 `[separation]`
- **Before any post mentions the product or the business at all,** check
  it against `../microsaas/docs/legal/specific-inventions-disclosure.md` §4
  (the scope of the employer disclosure) and the current gate states in
  `../microsaas/docs/opportunity-plan.md` §6. Don't rely on memory or on
  this file for gate states. They change. `[separation]`
- **The persona is mine; the company must outlive me.** *Mister Inevitable*
  is a person's voice and isn't transferable. If business traffic ever
  points here, the domain lives in a documented registrar account a
  successor can reach. ← `voice-and-messaging.md` §misterinevitable,
  item 4 `[separation]`
- **The blog is not product research.** I don't write posts to test
  messaging or harvest validation. If a post later turns out useful to the
  business, that's fine, but it wasn't written for that.
  `[separation][register]`

## 6. Process

- **The repository is public.** `ideas/`, drafts, and this file aren't
  built into the site, but anyone can read them on GitHub. Nothing goes
  into this repo, published or not, that can't be public: no real name
  for her, no medical records, no addresses, schools or therapists.
  `[process][her]`
- **Claude-drafted text is marked until it's mine.** Any draft Claude
  writes in my voice carries a notice at the top saying so, and isn't
  published until I've rewritten it as my own.
  ← `../microsaas/docs/vision/voice/adams-letter.md` (the notice pattern)
  `[process]`
- **Nothing auto-publishes.** A post goes live only when I move it into
  `src/_posts/` and push. ← microsaas ledger §5 *the parent's hand on everything
  generated* `[process]`
- **Nothing is pushed without my permission** _(Adam, 2026-10-02)_. Every
  push to `main` deploys the site, so a push is a publication. Commits
  stay local until I've reviewed what they contain and said to push.
  Permission covers one push, not the rest of a session.
  This applies to every branch and to force pushes. `[process]`
- **Every idea names its source.** When an idea comes from a microsaas
  doc, the idea file cites the path, so the argument doesn't have to be
  rebuilt. `[process]`
